## Writing bar for instruction text

- Be terse and direct, and state the result, the audience, the constraints and what counts as done. <!-- O1 -->
- State each rule once, where it is needed, with its reason beside it: the reason lets the reader apply the rule beyond its examples, and a second copy can drift from the first.
- State outcomes and boundaries, not step-by-step procedure, and leave out what the model does by default; every extra line dilutes the ones that matter. <!-- O2 -->
- Phrase rules positively and calmly, as what to do; capital-letter emphasis tends to cause overreach. State a real boundary as the action to take: leave VCS writes to the root, which owns the branch and PR record. A decision boundary names what the reader decides, what it settles by looking and what stays with the user. <!-- O3 -->
- Give each policy or invariant one owner, preserve the user's and any governing authority, keep what the runtime enforces in code or tool schemas rather than in prose, and put specialized steps in a skill. <!-- O16 -->
- Add no filler sections, redundant summaries or boilerplate to a written deliverable.
